# Case 01 — History-Taking Checklist (scoring content)

**Use:** This is the "did the student ask the right questions?" key. The feedback engine checks the interview transcript against these items and reports which were covered, which were missed, and — critically — whether any red flags were elicited. Pair it with the Calgary-Cambridge communication rubric (which scores *how* the student communicated); this checklist scores *what* they covered.

Each item is scored: **covered / partially covered / missed.**

---

## A. Characterise the pain (SOCRATES)

- [ ] **Site** — asked where the pain is.
- [ ] **Onset** — asked when and how it started (and what they were doing).
- [ ] **Character** — asked what the pain feels like (pressure vs sharp vs burning).
- [ ] **Radiation** — asked whether it spreads anywhere.
- [ ] **Associated symptoms** — asked about breathlessness, sweating, nausea (see section B).
- [ ] **Timing** — asked whether it's constant or comes and goes, and how long.
- [ ] **Exacerbating / relieving** — asked what makes it better or worse.
- [ ] **Severity** — asked to rate the pain.

## B. Associated cardiac symptoms (red-flag screen)

- [ ] Shortness of breath.
- [ ] Sweating / clamminess (diaphoresis).
- [ ] Nausea or vomiting.
- [ ] Palpitations.
- [ ] Light-headedness, dizziness, or fainting.

## C. Cardiac risk factors

- [ ] Smoking history.
- [ ] High blood pressure.
- [ ] Diabetes.
- [ ] High cholesterol.
- [ ] Family history of heart disease (and age of onset).
- [ ] Prior cardiac events or similar episodes.

## D. Relevant background

- [ ] Past medical history.
- [ ] Current medications.
- [ ] Allergies.
- [ ] Social history (alcohol, activity level, occupation).

## E. Patient perspective (ICE)

- [ ] **Ideas** — asked what the patient thinks is going on.
- [ ] **Concerns** — asked what they're worried about.
- [ ] **Expectations** — asked what they were hoping for from the visit.

## F. Clinical reasoning and safety (the part that matters most)

- [ ] Recognised the presentation as **possible acute coronary syndrome**.
- [ ] Treated it as **time-critical / an emergency** rather than routine.
- [ ] Named the immediate next steps: **ECG** and **troponin**.
- [ ] Did **not** reassure the patient home or dismiss the symptoms.

---

## Scoring weights (suggested, tune to taste)

| Section | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A. Pain characterisation | 20% | Core history skill. |
| B. Associated symptoms | 20% | These are the red flags. |
| C. Risk factors | 15% | Establishes pre-test risk. |
| D. Background | 10% | Completeness. |
| E. ICE | 10% | Patient-centred communication. |
| F. Reasoning & safety | 25% | The single most important dimension — missing ACS is the failure mode this whole tool exists to catch. |

**Equity note for the engine:** run this identical checklist against each demographic variant. Any difference in section F (reasoning & safety) across variants — same facts, different patient — is the flag the equity check reports as a teaching signal, not a verdict.
